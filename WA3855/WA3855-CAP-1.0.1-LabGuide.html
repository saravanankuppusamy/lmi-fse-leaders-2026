WA3855-CAP Lab Guide

Phase 1 Capstone Project for Leaders

Phase 1 Capstone — Lead & Assemble the Evergreen Quote Delivery

Lab 1

In this capstone, you will spend four days as the Delivery Lead for Evergreen Insurance Quote — a small web app of the kind the engineer track builds. You start from a starter project provided in your lab kit (a basic HTML page) and, over the week, expand it into a working Evergreen Quote app by assembling the provided pieces. You will lead a simulated four-day delivery week while you do it. On Friday you will present what your team delivered, run a lessons-learned review, and assess your peers.

You will not write code from scratch. Every coding step in this capstone is an assembly step: push the starter to your own repo, branch, drop in a provided partial, configure a class, wire a provided function, enable a workflow, open a PR. The skill being practiced is leading delivery — not authoring software.

Writing code is never required to complete this capstone. But if you want to get hands-on, several assembly blocks include a short, clearly-marked optional challenge — a small, self-contained change you can try. Across the week these cover adding a nav link (HTML), recoloring the brand (CSS), adding a coverage type (JavaScript), and adding a CI step (YAML). They are entirely optional; skipping them costs you nothing and does not affect completion.

 Tip

This capstone is the culmination of every Phase 1 topic — command line, Git, GitHub, HTML, CSS, Flexbox/Bootstrap, JavaScript, and CI/CD. You will touch each one as a leader, not as an engineer.

1.1. Who You Are This Week

You are the Delivery Lead for Evergreen Insurance Quote. You begin with a starter project in your lab kit — a basic HTML page that represents where an engineering team would hand off. You are not managing a roomful of peers. You are leading a delivery effort on a product — and getting hands-on by expanding that starter, assembling the provided pieces into a working app, not by writing code from scratch. Expect to put pieces together as well as lead.

  • You may work solo or paired (team of two). Choose Monday morning.

  • The instructor plays the project sponsor / stakeholder — bring questions to them when a real PM would.

  • The cohort runs team meetings (daily check-in, planning, review, lessons-learned) together, with a different learner leading each day.

 Note

"Assembly, not authoring." If you ever feel you need to write a function or hand-craft CSS, stop — the piece you need is already in your starter kit under files/.

1.5. Day 1 (Mon) — Kickoff, Planning & Setup

Goal for the day: by end of Monday, the cohort knows what Evergreen Quote is, you have a vision brief, a prioritized task list on a GitHub board, a delivery goal, and the repository running locally.

1.5.1. Daily Check-in — 10:00

This is the first check-in, so it doubles as introductions. Today’s meeting lead (volunteer or assigned by the instructor) asks each learner the three classic questions, with a Day-1 twist:

  • What did you do before this week to prepare?

  • What are you doing today?

  • What is already blocking you (access, tooling, pairing decisions)?

 Tip

Daily check-ins should take 15 minutes for the whole cohort. If a topic needs more than a sentence, take it offline — say "let’s take that after the check-in."

1.5.2. Morning Block — Lead

  1. Read the engineering team’s work. Open the engineering capstone brief in your lab kit at ~/LabFiles/wa3855-cap-phase-1-capstone-project-leaders/starter/CAPSTONE-BRIEF.md. You are not implementing it — you are leading a team that is. Skim the brief, the starter README.md, and the file structure. Form opinions: what’s the user value? what’s risky? what would you cut?

  2. Draft your vision brief. Open templates/vision-brief.md from the kit and copy it into your own delivery-leadership-package/vision-brief.md. Fill it in. A vision brief answers four questions in one page:

    • Who is the customer?

    • What pain does Evergreen Quote remove?

    • What does "good" look like at end of the week?

    • What are we explicitly not doing this week?

  3. Pair decision. By 11:30, tell the instructor whether you are working solo or paired, and (if paired) with whom.

1.5.3. Afternoon Block — Assemble (Environment & Task List)

  1. Create your project repository on GitHub and push the starter. Copy the starter project out of your lab kit, add the empty delivery-leadership-package/ scaffold for your written artifacts, put it all under version control, and push it to a new GitHub repo of your own. The gh CLI is pre-authenticated on your VM.

    cp -r ~/LabFiles/wa3855-cap-phase-1-capstone-project-leaders/starter ~/evergreen-quote-delivery
cd ~/evergreen-quote-delivery
cp -r ~/LabFiles/wa3855-cap-phase-1-capstone-project-leaders/delivery-leadership-package .
git init -b main
git add .
git commit -m "Initial commit: Evergreen Quote starter"
gh repo create evergreen-quote-delivery --private --source=. --remote=origin --push
code .

    Why this step: This repo on GitHub is your team’s work. From here on you are operating on it the way any delivery lead operates on a real team repo — through Git and GitHub, not by opening files manually.

  2. Run it once so you know it works on Day 1. Open index.html with Live Server (right-click → Open with Live Server). Confirm the page loads in the browser. If anything fails, that is your first risk — log it.

  3. Create your delivery branch.

    git checkout -b delivery/lead

    You will assemble everything onto this branch and merge to main via PR on Day 4.

  4. Set up your GitHub Project board. In a browser, go to your evergreen-quote-delivery repo on github.com, click Projects → New project → Board. Create three columns: To Do, In Progress, Done.

  5. Create your tasks as GitHub Issues and drop them on the board. Suggested starter set (you may add more):

    • [FE] Assemble homepage from provided partials

    • [FE] Apply theme + make responsive

    • [FE] Add testimonials section

    • [LOGIC] Wire provided quote-calculation snippet

    • [CI] Enable GitHub Actions workflow

    • [REVIEW] Open PR from delivery/lead to main

      Each issue needs done criteria — bullet points the work must satisfy to be "done."

  6. Write the delivery goal. Open templates/delivery-goal.md, copy to delivery-leadership-package/delivery-goal.md, and write one sentence the whole team could repeat back. Example shape: "By Thursday EOD, an assembled, themed, responsive Evergreen Quote app with the wired quote calculator is merged to main via a reviewed PR with a green CI run."

1.5.4. Wrap — 16:00

  1. Commit and push your delivery-leadership package so far:

    git add delivery-leadership-package/
git commit -m "Day 1: vision, delivery goal, draft task list"
git push -u origin delivery/lead

  2. Mid-afternoon check-in with the instructor. Share your vision brief and delivery goal out loud. Expected feedback: the goal should pass the "can a stranger repeat it back?" test.

1.6. Day 2 (Tue) — Build the Front End & Steer

Goal for the day: the page looks like Evergreen Quote, responds on mobile, and you have a roadmap + risk register + one decision memo on file. An inject lands after lunch and your day changes.

1.6.1. Daily Check-in — 10:00

A new meeting lead runs the check-in today. Three questions: yesterday / today / blockers. Keep it to 15 minutes.

1.6.2. Morning Block — Assemble (HTML)

  1. Open the starter kit’s HTML partials.

    ls ~/LabFiles/wa3855-cap-phase-1-capstone-project-leaders/html-partials

    You should see header.html, hero.html, quote-form.html, testimonials.html, footer.html, and index-skeleton.html.

  2. Assemble index.html. Open index-skeleton.html from the kit — it is the page skeleton with comment markers like <!-- INSERT: header -→. Copy it into your repo as index.html (overwriting). Then, for each marker, paste the contents of the matching partial in its place.

     Note

    You are not writing HTML. You are pasting provided HTML in the right slots and saving.

  3. Open it in Live Server and confirm all sections render: header, hero, quote form, testimonials, footer.

Example 1. Optional challenge — add a nav link (no code experience needed)

Want to feel what your engineers do? Add an About link to the header.

  1. In index.html, find the assembled navigation list — the <ul class="navbar-nav …​"> containing Home, Coverage, Claims, and Contact.

  2. Copy one <li class="nav-item">…​</li> line and paste it as a new line in the list.

  3. Change its text to About and its href to #footer.

  4. Reload in Live Server — your About link should appear in the nav and jump to the footer when clicked.

This is optional. Skipping it costs you nothing.

1.6.3. Morning Block — Assemble (CSS + Responsive)

  1. Apply the provided theme. Copy theme/theme.css from the kit into your repo’s css/ folder (create the folder if needed). In index.html, add a <link> tag pointing to css/theme.css after any existing stylesheet link.

  2. Apply provided Bootstrap classes in your HTML partials where marked <!-- CONFIG: add bootstrap class here -→. The kit’s README lists which class to use where — you are configuring, not authoring CSS.

  3. Resize the browser to phone width (≈375px). The page should reflow without horizontal scroll. If it doesn’t, that’s an issue — log it.

Example 2. Optional challenge — rebrand the color (no code experience needed)

The whole theme is driven by a few CSS variables. Change one and watch the page shift.

  1. Open css/theme.css and find --evg-green: #0f5132; near the top.

  2. Replace the hex value with a different color (try #1565c0 for blue or #6a1b9a for purple).

  3. Reload — the hero, buttons, and result box all pick up your new color, because they reference that one variable.

Bonus: the file darkens the button on hover via --evg-green-dark. Change that value too and watch the hover shade follow.

For this optional challenge only you may edit theme.css; otherwise leave it as provided.

1.6.4. Afternoon Block — Lead

  1. Draft your risk register. Copy templates/risk-register.md into delivery-leadership-package/. Identify at least 5 risks. Each row needs owner, likelihood (L/M/H), impact (L/M/H), mitigation, trigger to escalate.

  2. Draft your roadmap (stretch) — what would the next few delivery rounds look like if this product kept going?

  3. Write one decision memo. Copy templates/decision-memo.md. Pick a real tradeoff you made today (e.g., which sections did you cut to hit the responsive goal? did you prioritize testimonials or the quote form?). Write it for a non-technical reader: context → options → recommendation → why.

1.6.5. Inject #1 — ~14:00

The instructor will hand you a one-page Inject at about 14:00. You will not have seen it in advance — reacting to it cold is part of the exercise.

  1. Re-prioritize. Move issues on your Project board to reflect the new reality. Log what dropped and why.

  2. Write a stakeholder status update. Copy templates/status-update.md. Two paragraphs: what shipped, what slipped, what’s next, what you need.

  3. Send it to the instructor (post in the cohort channel and tag them).

1.6.6. Wrap — 16:00

  1. Commit and push:

    git add .
git commit -m "Day 2: assembled FE, themed, responsive; risk register; decision memo; inject #1 response"
git push

  2. Mid-afternoon check-in. The instructor will ask: did your status update name what you need from them, or just describe the problem? Iterate if needed.

1.7. Day 3 (Wed) — Wire the Logic, Oversee Quality & AI

Goal for the day: the Calculate Premium button on your assembled page returns a real number from the provided quote-calculation snippet, the GitHub Actions CI workflow is enabled and producing a result on every push, you have used Copilot for one assembly step and written a one-paragraph critique of its output, and you have made a documented go/no-go call based on the CI result. Inject #2 lands in the afternoon.

1.7.1. Daily Check-in — 10:00

New meeting lead. Same three questions. Today emphasize blockers — Day 3 is when reality bites.

1.7.2. Morning Block — Assemble (JavaScript)

  1. Locate the provided snippet.

    cat ~/LabFiles/wa3855-cap-phase-1-capstone-project-leaders/js-snippets/quote-calc.js

    This file defines a calculatePremium(coverageType, coverageAmount) function. You will wire it up — you will not modify it.

  2. Drop the snippet into your repo as js/quote-calc.js (create the js/ folder if needed).

  3. Wire it. In index.html, before </body>:

    • Add <script src="js/quote-calc.js"></script>

    • Add a second <script> block (also provided in the kit’s README) that listens to the form’s submit event, reads the inputs, calls calculatePremium(…​), and writes the result into the #premium-value element.

       Note

      The kit’s README has the exact wiring snippet to paste. You are not writing the event handler — you are pasting it in the right place.

  4. Configure values. The snippet has a ratePerThousand object at the top. The instructor will tell you Monday’s "project sponsor" rate decision. Update only the values to match — do not refactor the function.

  5. Test it. Reload in Live Server. Submit the form. A dollar amount should appear.

Example 3. Optional challenge — add a coverage type (a little JavaScript + HTML)

Add a Condo coverage option end-to-end.

  1. In js/quote-calc.js, add one line to the ratePerThousand object: condo: 0.7,

  2. In index.html, find the coverage <select id="coverageType"> and add a new option: <option value="condo">Condo</option>

  3. Reload, choose Condo, set an amount, and submit — you should get a premium for the new type.

You changed the data and the form; the provided function handled the rest. Optional, but satisfying.

1.7.3. Morning Block — Assemble (CI/CD)

  1. Enable the workflow. Copy workflows/ci.yml from the kit into .github/workflows/ci.yml in your repo. Commit and push:

    mkdir -p .github/workflows
cp ~/LabFiles/wa3855-cap-phase-1-capstone-project-leaders/workflows/ci.yml .github/workflows/
git add .github/workflows/ci.yml
git commit -m "Enable CI workflow"
git push

  2. Observe the run. In a browser, open your repo → Actions tab. A run should appear within ~30 seconds. Watch it complete. Read the result. If it’s red, read the logs and identify in plain English what failed — you are not fixing it as code; you are reading it as a leader.

Example 4. Optional challenge — add a CI step (a little YAML)

Add your own step to the pipeline.

  1. Open .github/workflows/ci.yml and find the steps: list.

  2. Add a new step at the end, matching the existing indentation:

          - name: Greeting
        run: echo "Evergreen Quote CI run by ${{ github.actor }}"

  3. Commit and push, then open the Actions tab and expand your Greeting step to read the message in the log.

You just edited a real CI pipeline. Optional — the provided workflow already passes without it.

1.7.4. Afternoon Block — Lead (AI tie-in)

  1. Use GitHub Copilot for one assembly step. Open VS Code, enable Copilot if it’s not already on, and ask it for help with one small thing — for example, "suggest two more testimonials matching the tone of the existing ones."

  2. Critique what it gives you. In one paragraph (~100 words), in your decision memo or as a stretch artifact, answer: what did Copilot get right? what did it get wrong? would you ship its output as-is? what would you change?

1.7.5. Inject #2 — ~14:00

The instructor will hand you a second inject at about 14:00 — typically a production incident scenario (the calculator returns a wrong premium, or CI on main has gone red after a merge, or a security flag from a dependency). As with the first, you will not have seen it in advance.

  1. Respond as a leader, not a fixer. Your job is to route the question, not answer it. Draft a one-paragraph message that:

    • Names what you observed.

    • Names what you’re asking for.

    • Names who owns the next step.

    • Offers air cover where appropriate.

  2. Make a go/no-go call. Copy templates/go-no-go.md. Based on the latest CI run plus the inject, decide: do we keep merging today, or do we hold? Write the decision, your reasoning, and what would change your mind.

1.7.6. Wrap — 16:00

  1. Commit, push, and check the CI result one more time:

    git add .
git commit -m "Day 3: wired calc; CI enabled; inject #2 response; go/no-go"
git push

  2. Mid-afternoon check-in. The instructor will ask: if you said no-go, what changed by tomorrow morning would flip it to go?

1.8. Day 4 (Thu) — Integrate, Finalize & Rehearse

Goal for the day: open the PR from delivery/lead into main, self-review and merge it, confirm the app still works end-to-end on main, build the delivery review presentation, and dry-run it once.

1.8.1. Daily Check-in — 10:00

New meeting lead. Today add a fourth question: what do you still need from anyone in the room before Friday?

1.8.2. Morning Block — Assemble (PR & Review)

  1. Open a pull request from delivery/lead into main:

    gh pr create --base main --head delivery/lead \
  --title "Evergreen Quote — assembled, themed, wired" \
  --body "See delivery-leadership-package/ for the full delivery output."

    (You can also open the PR in the GitHub UI if you prefer.)

  2. Self-review the diff in the Files changed tab. Read it the way you would read a teammate’s PR. Leave at least one inline comment that asks a question rather than commands a change.

  3. (Pairs only) Have your pair leave one inline comment and approve.

  4. Watch CI run on the PR. It must be green to merge. If it is red, read the logs out loud to your pair (or to yourself) and decide what to do — the answer might be "ask the engineering team," not "fix it myself."

  5. Merge the PR. Use the Squash and merge option. Delete the branch.

  6. Re-verify. Pull main locally and open the page in Live Server one more time. Confirm everything still works after the merge.

1.8.3. Afternoon Block — Lead (Consolidate & Rehearse)

  1. Consolidate the delivery-leadership package. Make sure every required deliverable from the table at the top is present, filled in, and committed to main.

  2. Build the delivery review presentation. Copy templates/delivery-review-outline.md to delivery-leadership-package/delivery-review-outline.md. Five slides (or one tight page):

    • Slide 1 — Delivery goal & whether we hit it.

    • Slide 2 — What shipped (demo screenshot of the assembled app).

    • Slide 3 — Two key decisions you made and why.

    • Slide 4 — Risks and how you handled the injects.

    • Slide 5 — What I’d do differently next round.

  3. Dry-run it once — out loud, to your pair or to yourself. Time it. Target 5 minutes.

1.8.4. Wrap — 16:00

  1. Commit and push every remaining artifact:

    git add .
git commit -m "Day 4: PR merged; delivery review presentation; package complete"
git push

  2. Confirm with the instructor: presentation slot booked for Friday, you know which order you’re presenting in.

1.9. Day 5 (Fri) — Delivery Review & Lessons Learned

Goal for the day: present your delivery, watch your peers present, and run a cohort lessons-learned review.

1.9.1. Delivery Reviews — 10:00 to ~13:00

Each learner (or pair) presents 5 minutes max, followed by 3 minutes of questions from "stakeholders" (instructors and peers). Your job during others' presentations is to be a respectful, curious stakeholder — not a competitor.

1.9.2. Lunch — 13:00

1.9.3. Lessons-Learned Review — 14:00

Cohort lessons-learned review using the went well / didn’t / change format. Each learner contributes at least one item to each column. The instructor leads today.

Then, in 10 minutes of quiet writing, each learner answers in their own notes:

  • What surprised me about leading delivery this week?

  • What habit do I want to take into Monday at work?

1.9.4. Debrief — 16:30

Instructor wraps the week tying back to the Phase 1 topic-to-activity map: every topic you saw in Phase 1 was something you used this week as a leader. Foreshadow Phase 2.

1.10. Edit Scope Reminders

  • You are assembling, not authoring. Completing this capstone never requires writing code — if an assembly step feels like it needs a new function or hand-crafted CSS, stop and ask the instructor where the provided piece is.

  • Optional challenges are the one exception. The blocks marked "Optional challenge" invite you to make a small change yourself (including lightly editing theme.css, quote-calc.js, or ci.yml). They are clearly labeled, entirely optional, and never required to finish.

  • The starter project in your lab kit is the engineering team’s work. Read it, lead around it, and assemble onto it; don’t refactor it.

  • Your starter kit at ~/LabFiles/wa3855-cap-phase-1-capstone-project-leaders/ is the source of every provided piece. If a step says "use the provided X," X is in there.
